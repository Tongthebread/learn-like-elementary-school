# Exercise Design Patterns

Read this reference when building exercises, hints, answer keys, or adaptive follow-up practice.

## Choose exercises by knowledge type

For a short lesson, use a compact diagnostic set. For exam preparation, build a larger bank in batches. Each batch should introduce meaningful variation in concepts, representations, reasoning, or context rather than cosmetic changes.

### Conceptual material

Move from recognizing a concept to distinguishing it, explaining cause and effect, applying it to a new case, and evaluating a claim. Good hard questions introduce a plausible distractor or require a justified comparison; they do not rely on obscure trivia.

### Quantitative material

Move from identifying variables and units to one-step substitution, multi-step calculation, method selection, and interpretation of the result. Change one source of difficulty at a time. Include enough data to solve the problem and avoid accidental clues in wording.

Verify arithmetic, signs, units, boundary conditions, and rounding in every solution. If multiple conventions can produce different answers, state the convention used.

### Procedural material

Move from ordering steps to completing a familiar procedure, selecting a procedure, diagnosing a broken procedure, and adapting it under a constraint. Assess why each step is needed, not only whether the sequence was memorized.

### Code and technical systems

Move from reading or predicting a small snippet to completing it, debugging it, designing a solution, and discussing tradeoffs. Keep examples runnable when tools are available. Separate syntax mistakes from conceptual mistakes in feedback.

### Humanities and social sciences

Move from identifying claims or evidence to explaining relationships, applying a framework, comparing interpretations, and constructing an evidence-based argument. Distinguish what the source says from the model's interpretation.

## Write useful distractors

For multiple-choice questions, make wrong answers reflect likely misconceptions, common calculation errors, reversed relationships, or neighboring concepts. Avoid joke answers, grammatical giveaways, and “all of the above” unless it serves a real diagnostic purpose.

## Build progressive hints

Use up to three hints when needed:

1. Point to the relevant concept or question to ask.
2. Suggest the first operation, distinction, or intermediate step.
3. Provide a nearly complete setup while leaving the final reasoning to the learner.

Do not make Hint 1 a disguised answer.

## Write solutions that teach

Each solution should include:

1. the target idea;
2. the reasoning or calculation steps;
3. the answer;
4. why a tempting wrong approach fails, when useful; and
5. a quick self-check such as units, scale, evidence, or consistency.

## Adapt the next question

- Correct with sound reasoning: keep the concept and add one complication.
- Correct with weak reasoning or a lucky guess: ask for an explanation or a parallel item.
- Small execution error: keep the difficulty and target that step.
- Wrong method: return to method selection with a contrasting pair.
- Missing prerequisite: teach it directly, then retry a simpler version.

Do not advance solely because the final answer matches.

## Scale practice without becoming repetitive

When the learner requests more exercises, distribute questions across a matrix of:

- topics from the source material;
- difficulty levels;
- recall, explanation, application, analysis, and transfer;
- guided versus independent work; and
- familiar versus unfamiliar contexts.

Avoid generating all questions at once when that would overwhelm the learner. Offer or deliver batches such as 5–10 focused questions, check the answers, and use the results to select the next batch.

For each expanded set, label:

- **Topic**
- **Difficulty**: foundational, standard, advanced, or challenge
- **Skill tested**
- **Expected time**
- **Prerequisites**, only when they are not obvious

Include cumulative questions from earlier material so success reflects retention rather than immediate imitation.
